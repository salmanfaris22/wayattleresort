## Campfire and barbecue nights

The campfire is the part of a stay most guests mention. On dry evenings the fire is lit on the lawn at about 7 pm, with tapioca and chilli chutney or tea. If you order a barbecue a day ahead, the kitchen grills chicken, fish or paneer over coals beside the fire. In the monsoon, when the lawn is wet, we skip the fire and serve snacks on the verandah instead.

## Trips we book for you

- **Chembra Peak trek.** Permits are issued by the forest department each morning and are limited, so we book the day before and pack breakfast.
- **Banasura Sagar Dam.** Speedboat and pedal-boat rides on the reservoir, with island views; under an hour away.
- **Jeep safaris.** Forest-department safaris at Muthanga and Tholpetty run mornings and afternoons; tickets sell out early.
- **Kuruva Island.** Bamboo rafts and forest trails on the Kabini river, closed in the peak monsoon.

The forest department sets the rules for treks and safaris and sometimes closes them at short notice because of rain or fire risk. We check the night before and change the plan if needed.

## Rainy-day ideas

Rain is part of a Wayanad stay. When it sets in, guests play carrom, book a massage, read on the verandah, or drive to Pookode Lake and En Ooru, which are both fine in light rain. A walk on the estate in a light shower, with umbrellas from the front desk, is one of the quietest hours you will have here.
