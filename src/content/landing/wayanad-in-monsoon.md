## What the monsoon is like

The south-west monsoon usually reaches Wayanad in early June and eases off by late September. The heaviest rain falls in July and August. Rain rarely falls nonstop all day: expect long showers, breaks of weak sunshine, and mist that drifts up from the valleys in the afternoon. Days are around 18 to 25 °C, so it is cool rather than hot.

## What is good about it

- **Waterfalls at full flow.** Soochipara and Meenmutty are at their most powerful, though the paths can close on very wet days.
- **The greenest landscape.** Tea, coffee and paddy fields are a bright, almost unreal green.
- **Fewer crowds.** Outside long weekends, popular spots are quiet.
- **Slow days.** Rain on a tiled roof, tea on the verandah and a book are the reason many people come.

## What to plan around

- Treks and river islands close; keep a flexible list of indoor and short outings.
- Ghat roads are slower and occasionally close for a few hours after slides; leave margin on travel days.
- Mobile networks can drop during storms, and short power cuts happen; ask us for resorts with power backup.
- Leeches appear on forest paths after rain. They are harmless but unpleasant; leech socks help.

## A rainy-day plan from Vythiri

Start with a plantation walk between showers, then drive ten minutes to Pookode Lake. After lunch, if the weather allows, visit Banasura Sagar Dam, where the reservoir and islands look dramatic under low cloud. End the day on the verandah. On a clear spell, drive to Soochipara Falls, but check that the path is open first.
